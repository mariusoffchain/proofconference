Introduction

Why this book

You are probably reading this because you attended PROOF in Vilnius, or because someone handed you a card with a QR code on it. Either way, welcome.

A conference lasts an afternoon. You listen to good talks, you agree with most of what is said, you promise yourself to change a few things, and on Monday morning ordinary life resumes. Habits, on the other hand, last for years: the messenger you use, the way you handle passwords, where you keep your savings. Those habits, far more than any talk, decide how exposed or how free you are.

This book connects the two. It takes the ideas from the stage and turns them into things you can actually set up, on your own phone and your own computer, with clear steps. You do not need a technical background. You need some curiosity and, for most chapters, less than an hour.

Nothing here asks you to change your whole life. It asks you to change one thing, and then, when you are ready, another.

Three proofs

The book has three parts, named after the three themes of the conference.

Proof of Privacy is about protecting yourself: your messages, your passwords, your browser, your email and your network connection. Privacy is the power to choose what you share and with whom. It comes first because everything else rests on it.

Proof of Autonomy is about depending less on gatekeepers: the few large companies that can close your account, change the rules overnight or lose your data. You will see how keys can replace accounts, how Nostr works, how to replace Big Tech services one at a time, and how to host a few things yourself.

Proof of Work is about Bitcoin: money that nobody can create more of to reduce its value, and that, when you hold the keys yourself, nobody can freeze. You will learn to hold it, back it up, use it and keep it private.

The three parts connect. Privacy protects you. Autonomy frees you from gatekeepers. Bitcoin applies the same ideas to money. One thought runs through all of them: whoever holds the keys is in control. This book helps you become that person.

How to use it

Chapters are independent. Read them in any order, skip what you already do, and come back later for the rest. Each part opens with a short essay on why the topic matters. After that, every chapter has the same structure: the chapter in brief, the problem, how it works, a choice of tools, a step-by-step tutorial, mistakes to avoid and resources to go further.

Each chapter ends with a short checklist. These feed the global checklist at the end of the book, where you see everything in one place. Every action carries an effort tag: (5 min), (1 hour) or (weekend). Start small. A five-minute action done today is worth more than a weekend project you never begin.

The book works offline once it has loaded, so a bad connection is not a problem. There is no account to create and nothing is tracked. Your progress is stored only in your own browser, on your own device. If you clear your browser data or switch to another device, it starts again from zero. That is the price of nobody else knowing what you have ticked.

A word on tools

For every need, this book lists several tools, never just one. None of them paid to be here. The conference has one sponsor, Bringin, and whenever it appears in these pages it is named as the sponsor and shown among other options. The same goes for any tool connected to one of our speakers.

Tools change. Apps get bought, abandoned or slowly made worse, and a recommendation that is right today may be wrong in three years. Principles stay. Three of them guide every choice in this book:

  • Open source. The code is public, so claims can be checked by people who do not work for the company.
  • No single point of failure. Prefer tools that do not depend on one company, one server or one country.
  • You hold the keys. If someone else can lock you out, it is not really yours.

Bitcoiners have a saying: "Don't trust, verify." It applies to this book too. Compare what you read here with other sources, test things with small steps, and if you find a mistake, tell us.
