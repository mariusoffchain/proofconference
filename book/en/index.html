<01> privacy

Proof of Privacy

Reduce what others can learn about you. Start with the basics: messages, passwords, browser.

Why it matters

Privacy is not about having something to hide. It is about deciding who gets to know what about you. Every message, search and login leaves traces that are collected, combined and sold, or leaked.

You do not need to disappear. A handful of habits removes most of the exposure: encrypted messages, unique passwords, a browser that works for you and less metadata on the network.

On stage at PROOF

  • SatoshineWhy Your Internet Security Depends on Your Keys

Key terms

End-to-end encryption
Only you and the person you talk to can read the message. Not even the service provider can.
Metadata
Not what you say, but who you talk to, when, from where and how often. Often more revealing than the content itself.
Two-factor authentication
A second proof on top of your password: a code from an app or a physical key.

What we recommend

  • Move your chats to Signal

    5 min

    End-to-end encrypted by default, open source, and it collects almost no metadata. Start with the five people you talk to most.

    Tools: Signal

  • Switch to a browser that blocks trackers

    5 min

    Your browser sees everything you do online. Pick one that blocks trackers by default, and change the default search engine.

    Tools: Firefox · Brave · Mullvad Browser

  • Use a password manager

    1 hour

    One strong master password, and a unique password for every site. A leak on one service no longer opens the others.

    Tools: Bitwarden · KeePassXC

  • Turn on two-factor authentication, without SMS

    1 hour

    SMS codes can be hijacked with a SIM swap. Use an authenticator app or a hardware key, starting with email, banking and exchanges.

    Tools: Aegis · Ente Auth · YubiKey

  • Use email aliases

    1 hour

    A different address for each service: you see who leaked your data, and your real address stays private.

    Tools: SimpleLogin · addy.io

  • Protect your network traffic

    1 hour

    Your internet provider and public wifi see which sites you visit. A trustworthy VPN or a mixnet hides that metadata.

    Tools: NymVPN · Mullvad VPN

<02> autonomy

Proof of Autonomy

Depend less on platforms that can lock you out. Own your identity, your data and your tools.

Why it matters

Most of our digital life runs on accounts that someone else can close. A platform can ban you, change its rules or shut down, and your contacts, your audience and your files go with it.

Autonomy means replacing accounts with keys, and rented services with tools you control. It does not happen in one day: one service at a time is enough.

On stage at PROOF

  • Alexis RousselHow the “Soldier-Citizen” Can Help Decentralize the World: Lessons from Russia’s Invasion of Ukraine
  • Marius Off-ChainRebuilding Social Media Without a Company in Charge using Nostr

Key terms

Private key
A secret number that proves you are you. Whoever holds it controls the identity or the money behind it.
Nostr
An open protocol for social media. Your identity is a key pair, and your posts travel through relays that anyone can run.
Self-hosting
Running a service on your own machine instead of renting it from a company.

What we recommend

  • Create your Nostr identity

    5 min

    A key pair instead of an account: no company can ban you or sell your audience. Follow the conference speakers to get started.

    Tools: Primal · Damus · Amethyst

  • Back up your private keys

    1 hour

    Your keys are your identity and your money. Write them down on paper, keep them in two places, never in a screenshot or in the cloud.

  • Replace one Big Tech service with an open alternative

    1 hour

    Pick one: email, maps, notes or cloud storage. Switching one service at a time is how the habit sticks.

    Tools: Proton Mail · Organic Maps · Nextcloud

  • Self-host your first service

    Weekend

    A small home server gives you your own cloud, your own photo backups and, later, your own Bitcoin node.

    Tools: Umbrel · Start9 · YunoHost

  • Take back control of your phone

    Weekend

    Review your app permissions today. When you are ready, move to a system without Google services.

    Tools: GrapheneOS · F-Droid

<03> work

Proof of Work

Money that nobody can freeze or debase, as long as you hold the keys.

Why it matters

Money is becoming a surveillance tool: every payment is tied to an identity, can be analysed and can be blocked. At the same time, inflation quietly erodes what you save.

Bitcoin is money without an issuer and without permission. But it only protects you if you hold the keys yourself: that is what this chapter is about.

On stage at PROOF

  • Linas K.CBDCs, KYC, and the Fight for Monetary Privacy
  • PiktasWhy Centralized Money Fails: How Bitcoin Became an Alternative

Key terms

Self-custody
You hold the keys to your bitcoin yourself. No company can freeze, lend or lose it for you.
Seed phrase
12 or 24 words that back up your whole wallet. Anyone who sees them can take everything.
Lightning
A payment network on top of Bitcoin: instant, nearly free, made for everyday amounts.
KYC and CBDC
KYC: mandatory identity checks by financial services. CBDC: digital money issued directly by a central bank. Together they make every payment traceable.

What we recommend

  • Install a self-custody Bitcoin wallet

    5 min

    Not your keys, not your coins. With a self-custody wallet, only you control your bitcoin.

    Tools: Phoenix · BlueWallet

  • Make your first Lightning payment

    5 min

    Instant and almost free. Try it at the conference: someone here will happily send you your first sats.

  • Back up your seed phrase properly

    1 hour

    12 or 24 words on paper or steel, offline, never photographed, never typed into a website. Then test the recovery.

  • Connect your euros and your bitcoin

    1 hour

    Bringin, the conference sponsor, links a euro IBAN to a self-custodial Lightning wallet, so you can move between euros and bitcoin without leaving coins on an exchange.

    Tools: Bringin

  • Move your savings to a hardware wallet

    Weekend

    For amounts you would not carry in your pocket, keep the keys on a dedicated offline device, bought directly from the manufacturer.

    Tools: BitBox02 · Coldcard · Trezor

  • Run your own node

    Weekend

    Verify your own transactions instead of trusting someone else's server. Don't trust, verify.

    Tools: Bitcoin Core · Umbrel · Start9
